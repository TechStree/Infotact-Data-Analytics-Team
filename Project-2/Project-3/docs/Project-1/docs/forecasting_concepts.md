# Forecasting Concepts

## What is Forecasting?

Forecasting is the process of predicting future values based on historical data. It helps businesses estimate future demand, sales, inventory requirements, and customer behavior.

## Why is Forecasting Important?

- Improves business planning
- Reduces inventory shortages
- Optimizes production
- Supports better decision-making
- Helps estimate future demand

## Types of Forecasting

### 1. Short-Term Forecasting
Predicts future values for a short period (days or months).

Example:
Predicting next month's product sales.

### 2. Long-Term Forecasting
Predicts future values for a longer period (one year or more).

Example:
Estimating product demand for the next five years.

## Common Forecasting Methods

### 1. Moving Average

Moving Average calculates the average of previous observations to predict future values.

Advantages:
- Easy to calculate
- Reduces random fluctuations

Disadvantages:
- Not suitable for seasonal data

### 2. ARIMA (AutoRegressive Integrated Moving Average)

ARIMA is a statistical forecasting model used for time-series data.

Advantages:
- Good for historical time-series data
- Widely used in forecasting

Disadvantages:
- Requires parameter tuning
- Works best with stationary data

### 3. Prophet

Prophet is a forecasting library developed by Meta (Facebook). It automatically handles trend and seasonality.

Advantages:
- Easy to use
- Handles missing values
- Suitable for business forecasting

Disadvantages:
- Less flexible for highly complex datasets

## Forecast Evaluation Metrics

### MAE (Mean Absolute Error)
Measures the average absolute prediction error.

### RMSE (Root Mean Square Error)
Measures the square root of the average squared prediction errors.

### MAPE (Mean Absolute Percentage Error)
Measures prediction error as a percentage.

## Business Applications

- Sales Forecasting
- Demand Forecasting
- Inventory Management
- Supply Chain Planning
- Production Planning

## Conclusion

Forecasting helps organizations make informed business decisions by predicting future demand using historical data. Choosing the right forecasting method depends on the nature of the data and business requirements.
